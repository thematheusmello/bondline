# Elemental analysis by weight

The public guide defines the Analysis window’s elemental analysis as a percent by weight for each element. Like the existing formula and mass fields, it uses the selected atoms or, without a selection, the whole drawing. [Guide v25, printed pp. 221–222](https://chemistry.beloit.edu/classes/programs/ChemDraw_25_manual.pdf)

Given isotope bin counts (n_i) and atomic weights (w_i), the contribution for element (E) is the sum of (n_i w_i) over its bins. Its percentage is 100 times this contribution divided by the whole scope’s molecular weight. All isotopes of the same element form one row, including isotope-unspecified inline H, physical H and deuterium. The existing molecular-weight calculation supplies and sums the individual weighted bins in identical order. A known isotope uses its tabulated mass. An unspecified isotope uses the conventional atomic weight. Net charge affects calculated exact mass only. [IUPAC mass fraction](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/M03722), [Bondline data, editions and charge convention](molecular-masses.md)

If any constituent lacks an atomic weight or numbered-isotope mass, the missing datum applies to the entire elemental analysis. The resolved formula remains available. A partial selection uses its full selected weight as the denominator, with the same assigned H and uncapped cut bonds as the existing formula. Outside the supported chemistry projection, unresolved H, stale epochs and the existing 4,096-item budget retain the analysis guards.

Bondline orders rows by increasing atomic number and reports at most 118 distinct element rows. The wire exposes unrounded contributions and percentages. Displayed percentages use two decimal places, independently rounded, and may sum to 99.99% or 100.01%. These order and display conventions are project choices. For ethanol the independently evaluated inputs are relative weights H 6.048, C 24.022 and O 15.999, totaling 46.069. The respective percentages are 13.13%, 52.14% and 34.73%. [CIAAW abridged standard weights](https://ciaaw.org/abridged-atomic-weights.htm)
