# Chemistry of one retained SMILES record

The analysis reads the parsed notation for the requested record. The decoded atoms and actual bonds determine the molecular composition. A dot starts a notation chain; ring closures can still connect the two sides. Component counts use graph traversal. One physical hydrogen is a drawn atom, including [2H] and [3H]. Bracketed atom H counts are literal and omission means zero. Positive numeric isotope labels remain distinct from natural abundance. The source distinction between zero and absent isotope cannot be represented by the existing composition type, so a zero isotope makes the analysis unavailable. [OpenSMILES 1.0, Organic Subset, Isotopes, More about Hydrogen, Disconnected Structures and Ring Numbers](https://github.com/opensmiles/OpenSMILES/blob/9240a2887158699503605bc838e1de9a9f7883f4/opensmiles.asciidoc).

For a bare nonaromatic organic-subset token, sum the integer orders of the atom’s actual bonds. The published normal valences are B 3, C 4, N and P 3 or 5, O 2, S 2, 4 or 6, and F, Cl, Br and I 1. Subtract the bond sum from the smallest normal valence at least as large. A sum above all normal valences gives zero implicit hydrogen. Neutral bare atoms have no isotope or formal charge. An explicit aromatic atom or bond has no integer-valence interpretation in this workflow. This convention is specific to OpenSMILES. Drawing normalization, editing, and foreign document defaults have their own source policies.

The reference uses RDKit 2026.09.2 in a separate process with normal sanitization and retained physical hydrogen atoms. Its descriptors for the three pinned records give C[13C]H6O, C7H14 and C4H9NaO. The latter two have two graph components. RDKit refuses the neutral four-bond N in N(F)(F)(F)F and the five-bond C in C(F)(F)(F)(F)F during sanitization. The published SMILES counting convention gives one or zero implicit H on the central atom, respectively. These cases test the specification directly and are excluded from claims of agreement with the sanitized RDKit molecule. Formula accounting does not assert chemical validity, assigned radical state or aromaticity. [RDKit molecular readers](https://www.rdkit.org/docs/source/rdkit.Chem.rdmolfiles.html) · [RDKit formula descriptor](https://www.rdkit.org/docs/source/rdkit.Chem.rdMolDescriptors.html#rdkit.Chem.rdMolDescriptors.CalcMolFormula).
