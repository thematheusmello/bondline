# Molecular weight and exact mass

The v25 guide describes whole-drawing, current-structure and partial-structure analysis. It distinguishes average atomic weights from masses of predominant nuclides and treats mass-to-charge distributions as a separate result. It names a 2019 average-weight compilation and an older NIST edition for isotopes. Bondline uses the explicit edition and charge conventions below. [Public guide, printed pp. 221–224](https://chemistry.beloit.edu/classes/programs/ChemDraw_25_manual.pdf)

* Molecular weight is the dimensionless sum of conventional atomic weights for isotope-unspecified atoms and relative isotope masses for numbered atoms. The feature uses the pinned dataset's single representative values for naturally variable elements, including Cl 35.45 and Ar 39.948. Natural samples can vary. CIAAW's 2024 abridged recommendations round Ar to 39.95. [IUPAC relative molecular mass](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/R05271), [CIAAW standard weights](https://ciaaw.org/atomic-weights.htm), [abridged tables](https://ciaaw.org/abridged-atomic-weights.htm)
* For monoisotopic exact mass, use the expressly numbered nuclide or the most abundant natural nuclide of each isotope-unspecified element. Every attached H already present in the composition counts as isotope-unspecified H. The result is in daltons. [IUPAC monoisotopic mass](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/12495), [NIST atomic masses and abundances](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Compositions/index.html)
* RDKit's average-weight descriptor sums the neutral-atom values. For calculated ionic exact mass, subtract formal net charge times the electron mass, 0.0005485799090441 Da. Both signs and multiple charges are covered. The approximation excludes electronic and chemical binding energies. The guide does not specify a charge correction, so this is a documented interoperability choice. [RDKit descriptors](https://www.rdkit.org/docs/source/rdkit.Chem.Descriptors.html), [NIST CODATA electron mass in u](https://physics.nist.gov/cgi-bin/cuu/Value?meu)
* All 118 elements have an identity. CIAAW gives standard atomic weights for 84. Tc, Pm, Po–Ac and Np–Og have no standard weight or representative natural isotope usable here. For these, natural weight and exact mass give separate unavailable reasons. A numbered, tabulated nuclide still supplies both masses. For an absent nuclide the result identifies the exact atomic and mass numbers. A zero-atom arithmetic composition sums to zero. The window reports an empty drawing separately.
* Both quantities share the formula's source coverage, selected atoms, resolved hydrogen counts and net formal charge. An incomplete fragment reports **mass contributions of the selected atoms**, including their assigned H and charge. A cut bond does not add a cap. Summing disconnected components includes them all.

## Data and repeatability

Bondline pins MIT-licensed elements_rs 0.2.8 without optional features. The archive SHA-256 is `e5834efe826a9a66b548e0887d9fc744fc8bb57065dcc6e5495359b25ab98319`. It contains 3,414 numbered nuclides across 118 elements. Missing or estimated and edition-dependent nuclides make this a calculated descriptor, without a universal experimental precision claim. In a comparison of 3,352 NIST all-isotope records, 3,264 identities overlap, 3,131 match within 1e-10 Da, 133 have different tabulated masses, and 88 occur only in that NIST edition. Another 150 occur only in the package. For example, NIST tabulates Og-293 while this package starts at Og-294. [Pinned package](https://docs.rs/elements_rs/0.2.8/elements_rs/), [source revision](https://github.com/earth-metabolome-initiative/elements-rs/tree/4e9a90bad24efaa3dfcdbd4fe7078c9725e3bb3f), [NIST all-isotope records](https://physics.nist.gov/cgi-bin/Compositions/stand_alone.pl?ele=&all=all&ascii=ascii2&isotype=all)

`cargo test -p bondline-chem --test molecular_masses` uses the repository's external RDKit interpreter through `BONDLINE_PYTHON`. Literal NIST/CODATA reference cases use 1e-9 Da or units of relative molecular weight. The whole-molecule RDKit tests distinguish typical nuclide differences below 1e-7 Da from identified edition differences. For Fe-56, RDKit 2026.09.2 uses 55.9349375 while NIST uses 55.93493633. Both calculations retain the same two-electron shift for Fe²⁺. For Tc-99 editions differ by 0.0000039 Da. Across all 84 natural elements the same nuclide number is required, with an additional mass agreement bound of 0.00005 Da. Absolute NIST cases independently detect an incorrect shared charge convention.

The two lockfiles pin four additional packages. elements_rs and strum are MIT. The build-only strum_macros is MIT and heck provides MIT OR Apache-2.0. Their upstream MIT notices are retained together. [Notices](../notices/molecular-masses/)

The offline table audit checks all 84 single weights against the published central value and uncertainty in the 2024 abridged CIAAW HTML, then measures the numbered-nuclide coverage and differences against the NIST all-isotope ASCII HTML. Run `python3 xtask/oracles/analysis_mass_tables.py PATH_TO_UNPACKED_ELEMENTS_RS CIAAW_HTML NIST_ASCII_HTML` after obtaining the linked editions. The reviewed CIAAW file had SHA-256 `f9e9554471749c55a624aec55151922470a7f4104c62811eb194fed9731b907d`, and the NIST file had SHA-256 `34fe6849481fbb47d841a3f266e3f3b150492a24a320c71500bce24ead4dfcfd`. Every conventional value passed the published uncertainty bound.
